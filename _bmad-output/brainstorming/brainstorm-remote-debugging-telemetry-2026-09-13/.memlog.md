- (technique by coach) Job to Be Done: Ask what the user is really hiring this to do, then ideate around that underlying job, not the feature assumed.
- (technique by coach) TRIZ Contradiction: Name the core contradiction between comprehensive evidence and family privacy, then seek a design that protects both.
- (technique by coach) Solution Matrix: Map evidence types against storage, transport, review, and privacy controls to find useful combinations and gaps.
- (technique by coach) Utopia vs Dystopia Split-Screen: Describe the telemetry system working beautifully and failing horribly, then keep the safeguards that survive both.
- (direction by user) Automatically record comprehensive debugging logs and analytics to a remote location for regular review of family-product issues.
- (insight by coach) The real job is to reconstruct why a family request failed, stalled, or behaved strangely without asking the family to reproduce it.
- (insight by coach) The Home repository already has low-cardinality Prometheus metrics and an ops Alloy-to-Grafana remote-write path; the missing layer is cross-surface correlation and automatic incident evidence. TUI diagnostics are currently content-safe and local.
- (idea by coach) Use an always-on content-safe incident envelope across TUI, phones, displays, Home, and Hermes bridge: correlation ID, endpoint and session fingerprints, phase transitions, durations, route, model/profile references as non-reversible IDs, byte counts, status, and typed failure reason—never prompts, transcripts, audio, keys, or credentials.
- (question by coach) Should actual prompts, transcripts, or audio ever leave the household automatically, or should those require a separate manual incident capture?
- (decision by user) Prompts, transcripts, and audio require a separate explicit incident capture; they do not leave the household automatically.
- (technique by coach) Switched from Job to Be Done to TRIZ Contradiction: seek a design that maximizes debugging evidence without maximizing private-data exposure.
- (insight by coach) The contradiction is not solved by choosing either total logs or no logs. Keep automatic telemetry rich in timing, state, and failure structure, then make sensitive evidence a bounded, visible, user-triggered incident bundle.
- (idea by coach) Use two tiers: an always-on safe event stream, plus an explicit incident capture tied to one device and current task/session, with a short time window, preview of what will be included, encryption, retention, and a visible upload status.
- (decision by user) An explicit incident capture may include a short pre-failure local ring buffer; the first recommendation is 60 seconds.
- (technique by coach) Switched from TRIZ Contradiction to Solution Matrix: map evidence, storage, transport, review, and retention choices against the debugging job.
- (insight by coach) The matrix points to three separate paths: low-cardinality metrics for trends, structured event logs for reconstruction, and encrypted incident bundles for selected content. One backend should not force all three into the same retention and access policy.
- (idea by coach) Use the existing ops host as the review boundary: Prometheus for time-series health, a structured log store for cross-device event timelines, and an encrypted incident-bundle store for explicit captures. Grafana can link the three by correlation ID without putting transcript or audio into metrics.
- (decision by user) Record the safe lifecycle for every turn, including successful turns, not only failures.
- (technique by coach) Switched from Solution Matrix to Utopia vs Dystopia Split-Screen: keep the review value of automatic evidence while testing how the system could become covert surveillance.
- (insight by coach) The good future has one correlation ID linking metrics, event timeline, health checks, and a user-approved incident bundle. The bad future has raw family conversations, credentials, high-cardinality labels, and permanent retention mixed into one searchable store.
- (constraint by coach) The collector must reject sensitive fields by schema, keep metrics and detailed logs under separate access and retention policies, encrypt incident bundles, audit access, and make deletion a real operation rather than a promise in a README.
- (idea by coach) Make every endpoint report a visible diagnostic state: safe telemetry on or off, last successful upload, queued events, last incident capture, retention deadline, and remote collector reachability. The household should know what the machine is sending.
- (decision by user) Use 30-day retention for safe metrics, 14-day retention for detailed event logs, and 7-day retention for incident bundles unless a user explicitly preserves one.
- (technique by coach) Convergence move: MoSCoW scoping for the household diagnostics capability.
- (decision by coach) Must: content-safe event schema, one correlation ID across endpoint/Home/Hermes, safe lifecycle events for every turn, remote metrics and structured logs, schema rejection of sensitive fields, encryption, access audit, visible endpoint diagnostic state, and fixed retention/deletion. Should: explicit incident capture for one device and task/session, 60-second pre-failure ring buffer, preview, encrypted bundle, upload status, and Grafana-linked review. Could: automatic failure prompts, anomaly detection, cross-device health summaries, and trend reports. Won't this time: automatic raw prompts/transcripts/audio upload, credentials or keys in telemetry, indefinite retention, or a new Hermes protocol channel.
- (direction by coach) Name the capability Household Diagnostics and Incident Review: automatic safe observability for every turn plus explicit, bounded capture when content-bearing evidence is needed.
- (insight by coach) Final synthesis: the household diagnostics capability is three linked but separately governed layers—automatic safe telemetry for every turn, remote metrics and structured timelines for review, and explicit encrypted incident bundles for selected private content. The one correlation ID makes them useful together without forcing the same privacy policy onto all three.
- (decision by user) Record Household Diagnostics and Incident Review in the canonical Home PRD and feature slate, with Slice D as the first implementation slice for its cross-surface foundation.
- (event by coach) Brainstorm converged and the accepted direction was handed off to the canonical Home PRD and feature slate.
