- (direction by user) Explore a real product direction for the household display, web/iPad interface, and TUI rather than treating them as isolated interface projects.
- (technique by coach) Started Job to Be Done: uncover the underlying household job beneath the current voice/display features.
- (idea by user) Move beyond chatbot back-and-forth: Hermes responses should become tappable choices and manipulable visualizations, with those manipulations sent back as input rather than requiring words.
- (insight by coach) The product direction is an agent that externalizes a situation into an operable interface; conversation is one input channel, not the whole product.
- (idea by user) The simplest valuable interaction is a response containing multiple options where the user chooses one through the UI instead of replying yes or no in language.
- (insight by coach) The first wedge is a shared decision primitive: prose explains options, each surface renders selectable options, and the chosen option returns as a structured interaction event.
- (idea by user) A touch surface could use press-and-hold on an option for ask-for-more-details; the TUI could map the same secondary intent to the right arrow while Enter performs the primary choice.
- (insight by coach) Shared interaction semantics should stay consistent across surfaces while gestures remain native: choose is primary, deepen/explore is secondary.
- (idea by user) Every option should support asking for more detail, while Hermes may emphasize that affordance for options that likely need exploration.
- (insight by coach) The interaction rule is universal inspectability with agent-ranked emphasis: Hermes can recommend depth but cannot hide exploration behind language.
- (question by user) Uncertainty remains about what form more-details exploration should take.
- (idea by coach) Seed concept: an option is a decision object exposing outcome, trade-offs, assumptions, and recommendation-changing factors; deeper layers remain manipulable objects rather than a wall of text.
- (direction by user) The recommendation-to-explorable-decision-object is a credible initial use case and a decent direction for the simple starting point.
- (technique by coach) Switched to Role Playing: test the decision-object direction against household stakeholders and each surface.
- (idea by user) A shared display must show which profile or person a decision object is for, because the object may not be intended for the person looking at it.
- (insight by coach) Audience and ownership are first-class state on shared display objects; physical visibility does not imply permission or relevance.
- (idea by user) The household display is shared, so Jensen can inspect a decision object even when it is addressed to Amanda.
- (insight by coach) The product should separate shared visibility from action authority: a household canvas can be transparent while commit identity remains explicit.
- (idea by user) On a shared household display, it is acceptable for Jensen to act on an object addressed to Amanda; physical tap authority cannot be reliably distinguished, so shared action is preferable.
- (insight by coach) Household collaboration should be permissive by default; actor identity may be recorded when known but should not gate ordinary actions on the shared surface.
- (idea by user) Some decisions on the shared display may require a passcode to commit, especially to keep child exploration from causing adult consequences.
- (insight by coach) Use soft exploration and hard commitment: let children inspect and manipulate safely, while consequence-bearing actions require confirmation or a household passcode.
- (question by user) The passcode boundary may be jointly set by household rules and Hermes risk assessment; the exact policy remains unresolved.
- (insight by coach) Safety principle: household policy sets the minimum protection; Hermes may add confirmation based on consequence, but must not weaken the floor silently.
- (technique by coach) Switched to SCAMPER Method: mutate the decision-object concept across substitute, combine, adapt, modify, repurpose, eliminate, and reverse lenses.
